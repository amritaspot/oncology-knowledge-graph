# Unstructured Notes & Narrative: OROPHARYNGEAL_SCC_HPV_POS

### metastasis_patterns_note
See ORAL_CAVITY_SCC for aggregated upper aerodigestive tract (UAT) data from Riihimäki 2018; these data should not be treated as an HPV-positive oropharyngeal SCC-specific per-organ metastasis distribution. That source does not separately report HPV-positive per-organ distributions. HPV-positive tumors have a distinct clinical course, often presenting in younger patients and generally having a better prognosis than HPV-negative tumors. Regional cervical lymph-node metastases are common at presentation. The lung is the most common distant metastatic site; bone and liver are also recognized sites. Some series describe late or unusual distant metastases, but these observations do not establish a uniform HPV-specific distribution.

### tumor_microenvironment.notes
An immune-rich TME is associated with favorable outcomes, and TIL density has shown independent prognostic associations in multiple cohorts. It may contribute to treatment sensitivity, but it does not by itself explain the survival advantage or reliably predict individual PD-1 inhibitor responsiveness. De-intensification trials are motivated principally by favorable outcomes and the opportunity to reduce treatment morbidity in carefully selected patients, not by immune infiltration alone. Compared with HPV-negative disease, the TME often reflects viral antigen-associated immune responses.

### cellular_states_and_lineage.notes
Distinct anatomic-molecular biology: tonsillar crypt reticulated epithelium is naturally permeable and specialized for interactions with immune cells. This anatomy is considered relevant to susceptibility to HPV-associated carcinogenesis, but it does not fully explain viral persistence. p16 overexpression resulting from E7-mediated RB pathway inactivation is an established diagnostic surrogate in oropharyngeal SCC, with recognized HPV/p16-discordant cases.
