# Unstructured Notes & Narrative: CHRONIC_EOSINOPHILIC_LEUKAEMIA

### heme_involvement_pattern.notes
Requires exclusion of reactive causes and other specific entities. Per current WHO/ICC frameworks, neoplasms with eosinophilia and tyrosine kinase fusions are classified separately, including PDGFRA (most commonly FIP1L1-PDGFRA), PDGFRB, FGFR1, and JAK2 rearrangements.

### tumor_microenvironment.notes
PDGFRA-rearranged disease, especially FIP1L1-PDGFRA, is exquisitely imatinib-sensitive; very low-dose imatinib is often sufficient and durable deep molecular remissions are common. FGFR1-rearranged disease is aggressive. Distinguishing CEL-NOS from idiopathic hypereosinophilic syndrome (HES) requires demonstration of clonality and exclusion of other defined molecular entities.

### cellular_states_and_lineage.notes
Distinct molecular entities with fusion-driven kinase activation targeting eosinophil lineage. End-organ damage from eosinophil infiltration (cardiac endomyocardial fibrosis, pulmonary infiltrates, neuropathy, skin, GI) is defining pathophysiology. PDGFRA-rearranged disease, especially FIP1L1-PDGFRA, is exquisitely imatinib-sensitive. FGFR1-rearranged disease represents aggressive stem cell disease with lymphoid and myeloid mixed features.
