# Unstructured Notes & Narrative: RECTAL_ADENOCARCINOMA

### metastasis_patterns.context
Frequencies among rectal cancer patients with recorded metastasis (~31% of the total 17,811 rectal cohort in the source study). Numerator = patients with metastasis to that organ; denominator = all patients with any recorded metastatic site.

### metastasis_patterns.organ_involvement.HEPATOBILIARY.notes
Liver; most common metastatic site.

### metastasis_patterns.organ_involvement.PULMONARY.notes
Lung; significantly more common than in colon cancer (OR 2.41 vs colon). Rectal venous drainage partly bypasses the portal system via the middle/inferior rectal veins, which is a commonly cited anatomic explanation for the higher lung-metastasis frequency.

### metastasis_patterns.organ_involvement.MUSCULOSKELETAL.notes
Bone; third most common site in rectal cancer, in contrast to peritoneum being third in colon. OR 1.38 vs colon.

### metastasis_patterns.organ_involvement.NEUROLOGIC.notes
Nervous system/brain; OR 1.49 vs colon.

### metastasis_patterns.organ_involvement.GASTROINTESTINAL.notes
Peritoneal spread markedly less common than in colon.

### tumor_microenvironment.notes
MSI-H/dMMR rectal cancer is highly responsive to neoadjuvant PD-1 blockade in small prospective studies; dostarlimab produced 100% clinical CR in the initial Cercek et al. cohort, enabling non-operative management in selected patients.

### cellular_states_and_lineage.notes
Same lineage biology as left-sided colon cancer. Anatomic location (proximity to sphincter and pelvic organs) drives distinct treatment paradigm (neoadjuvant CRT/TNT, TME surgery). MSI-H/dMMR subset responds dramatically to neoadjuvant immunotherapy (Cercek/dostarlimab), enabling non-operative management in selected patients.
