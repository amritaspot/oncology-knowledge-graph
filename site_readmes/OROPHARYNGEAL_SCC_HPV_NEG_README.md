# Unstructured Notes & Narrative: OROPHARYNGEAL_SCC_HPV_NEG

### metastasis_patterns_note
See ORAL_CAVITY_SCC for the cited aggregated UAT data from Riihimäki 2018; these should not be treated as HPV-negative oropharyngeal SCC-specific per-organ estimates. Regional spread commonly involves cervical lymph nodes, particularly levels II–III; retropharyngeal nodes may also be involved. The lung is the most common distant metastatic site, with bone and liver also recognized sites. Pulmonary lesions require distinction from second primary lung cancers, particularly in tobacco-exposed patients. HPV-negative disease generally has worse prognosis than HPV-associated disease, but aggregate UAT data do not establish an HPV-negative-specific organ distribution.

### tumor_microenvironment.notes
Frequent TP53 and CDKN2A alterations characterize HPV-independent biology. A uniformly lower neoantigen load is not established: tobacco-associated tumors can have substantial somatic mutation burdens, whereas HPV-associated tumors additionally express viral antigens. PD-L1 expression is variable. Checkpoint inhibitors can benefit both HPV-negative and HPV-associated HNSCC; HPV status alone is not a validated treatment-selection biomarker or a reliable basis for asserting inferior immunotherapy efficacy.

### cellular_states_and_lineage.notes
Distinct from HPV-associated oropharyngeal SCC despite anatomic overlap. Frequent TP53 mutation and CDKN2A inactivation disrupt tumor-suppressor pathways, rather than HPV E6/E7-mediated effects; TP53 mutations may have loss-of-function, dominant-negative, or gain-of-function consequences. Other recurrent alterations in HPV-negative HNSCC include CCND1 amplification, PIK3CA-pathway alterations, and NOTCH1 alterations, but their frequencies should not be assumed to be identical in the oropharyngeal subset. No defining recurrent driver fusion is established for conventional HPV-negative oropharyngeal SCC. It shares important biological features with other HPV-independent UAT SCCs. AJCC 8 uses separate staging for p16-positive and p16-negative oropharyngeal SCC, recognizing their clinical distinction while using p16 as an imperfect surrogate for HPV association.
