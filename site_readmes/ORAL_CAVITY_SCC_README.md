# Unstructured Notes & Narrative: ORAL_CAVITY_SCC

### metastasis_patterns.aggregation_note
UAT is an aggregated anatomical category, not an oral-cavity-SCC-specific cohort. The supplied description groups oral cavity, oropharyngeal, hypopharyngeal, laryngeal, lip and sinonasal cancers; the exact included subsites and whether the cohort is restricted to SCC require confirmation from the original source. HPV-positive and HPV-negative oropharyngeal disease must not be assumed to have been separately identified. The supplied cohort counts and percentages are retained as unverified source transcriptions, not independently verified oral cavity SCC estimates. The denominator and ascertainment period also require source confirmation; these values must not be interpreted as the percentage of all oral cavity SCC patients developing each metastasis. Organ involvement can overlap. Subtype-specific patterns are not resolved. Regional cervical lymph-node spread, the principal metastatic route in oral cavity SCC, is distinct from the distant-organ involvement listed here.

### tumor_microenvironment.notes
Tobacco exposure can be associated with higher TMB and predicted neoantigen burden, but this is not a uniform feature of oral cavity SCC. Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) and tumour-associated macrophages (TAMs), including M2-like populations, can promote invasion and immune suppression; both populations are heterogeneous. PD-L1 can be expressed on tumour and immune cells. CPS is the number of PD-L1-staining tumour cells, lymphocytes and macrophages divided by the number of viable tumour cells, multiplied by 100.

### cellular_states_and_lineage.notes
NOTCH1 mutations are reported in approximately 15-20% of some HNSCC sequencing cohorts, with substantial population and site variation; many are loss-of-function alterations affecting squamous differentiation. TP63 and SOX2 amplification or overexpression can support basal/squamous lineage programs but is not universal. Other established recurrent alterations in oral cavity SCC include CCND1 amplification, PIK3CA mutation or amplification, and mutations involving FAT1, CASP8 and HRAS; frequencies vary across cohorts. Conventional oral cavity SCC has no established recurrent, diagnostically defining driver fusion. Areca nut exposure, often through betel quid with or without tobacco, causes oral submucous fibrosis, a potentially malignant fibrotic disorder particularly prevalent in South and Southeast Asia.
